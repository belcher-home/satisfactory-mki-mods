# MKI Tools

Tools for shaping the world, by MidKnight Industries. `MKITools.uplugin` declares `SemVersion` (currently `0.4.0`). It requires only SML `^3.12.0`; it does not need MKI Roads or MKI Bridge, and neither of them needs it.

## Terrain dismantle mode

The dismantle gun gets a third mode, **Terrain**, after the vanilla Default and Blueprint modes. Switch to it the way you switch to Blueprint dismantle. In Terrain mode the gun aims only at level rocks: the rock under the crosshair is outlined, and holding fire on it for half a second removes it (the dismantle progress bar shows the hold). In the Default and Blueprint modes nothing changes.

- **What it removes:** plain rocks, arches, cliffs and boulders placed in the map (the engine's own `StaticMeshActor`, static, placed in a level). It never touches buildables, resource deposits, foliage (the chainsaw removes it), resource nodes and their meshes, the landscape, or anything spawned while playing. Reach is 150 m.
- **How:** a removed rock is hidden and loses its collision. It is never deleted, so it can always be restored exactly. There is no refund.
- **Nobelisk rocks:** a rock a nobelisk can blow up (`AFGDestructibleActor`, often over a resource node or a cave entrance) is outlined too, and holding fire breaks it the way a nobelisk does: the game's own fracture, with its effects, uncovering what it covered, saved and replicated by the game. It cannot be restored, as after a nobelisk.
- **Saves and streaming:** the removed rocks are saved with the game. The world streams rocks out and back in as you move, and every time an area loads, its removed rocks are hidden again.
- **Multiplayer:** the host keeps the list and every client applies it. A client's click is checked again on the host (the same rules, and the player's reach).
- **From far away:** the world partition draws distant areas as merged HLOD meshes. Where the HLOD keeps a removed rock as an instance of the same mesh, that instance is hidden too, again each time the HLOD streams in. Where the HLOD drew the rock as a simplified mesh of its own (one that fits inside the rock), that mesh is hidden too. Where a rock is merged into a larger far-view mesh with the terrain around it, it may still show from far away; it is gone (and has no collision) once its area loads around you.
- **Restoring:** MKI Bridge's `POST /v1/terrain/restore-rock` (and the MCP `terrain_rocks` tool, `action: "restore"`) brings a rock back by its actor name; `GET /v1/terrain/removed-rocks` lists them.

## How it works

- `UMKITerrainDismantleMode` is a native dismantle mode descriptor; the module appends it to `UFGBuildGunStateDismantle::GetSupportedBuildModes`.
- In Terrain mode, hooks on the dismantle state's `TickState` and `PrimaryFire` run `FMKITerrainDismantle` instead of vanilla targeting: a trace from the player's view, `FMKITerrainRules`, the dismantle outline, and on click `UMKIToolsRemoteCallObject::Server_RemoveRock`. `Config/AccessTransformers.ini` makes `FMKITerrainDismantle` a friend of the vanilla state, so it can clear the vanilla aim when Terrain mode takes over.
- `AMKITerrainSubsystem` (an SML mod subsystem, spawned on the server and replicated) owns the saved list and applies it to every level the world adds. It also answers MKI Bridge, which calls its `Bridge*` functions by reflection.

## Area dismantle mode

The dismantle gun gets a fourth mode, **Area** (#402), after Terrain. It dismantles everything inside a cube in one go, with the game's own refunds.

- **The cube:** aim at a building (any item or foundation) or the ground. On a building, the cube's bottom centre is the building's centre at its lowest point, so the building itself is inside, with everything at its level. On the ground, the cube stands on the point you aim at. The cube is aligned to the world axes and grows upward. On an item (a machine, a container, a belt support) the bottom is 10 cm above the item's base, so the floor it stands on stays. What lies wholly below its bottom face, such as the foundation under the one you aim at, stays. Belts and supports drawn by the game's instance manager are found through the hit, so aiming at a belt works.
- **Size:** the scroll wheel (or the d-pad) sets the edge: 16, 32, 48, 64, 80 or 96 m. The height equals the edge.
- **What goes:** everything that reaches more than 1 cm into the cube, with no filters: the building aimed at, machines, walls, foundations, and belts, pipes, rails, roads and power lines that only cross the cube. Belts, pipes, rails and roads are tested along their spline, which may lie up to 50 cm below the bottom face (so the aimed belt and the belts beside it go), and power lines between their two ends. Every belt support holding a taken belt's end goes with it, even one standing below the cube. What the game would refuse to dismantle is left alone. Vehicles are never buildings, so they never go.
- **Preview:** the cube is drawn as an outline. Up to 300 of the selected buildings get the game's dismantle outline (lightweight foundations and walls are counted, but not outlined). A line under the crosshair shows the count and the refund.
- **Confirm:** hold fire for a second. The game's progress bar shows the hold.
- **Refunds:** the selection is sent to the game's own dismantle (`Server_DismantleActors`), 200 buildings per tick, highest first, so nothing is left standing on what went before it. The game checks each piece and refunds it as a normal dismantle does: to the inventory, then a dismantle crate.

How it works: `UMKIAreaDismantleMode` is a dismantle mode descriptor whose multi-dismantle limit is the batch size. While the mode is selected, hooks on the dismantle state's `TickState` and `PrimaryFire` run `FMKIAreaDismantle`: a trace to the aimed building (a lightweight instance found by its shape) or upward-facing ground, the cube and its selection from `FMKIAreaRules` (pure geometry: bottom centre, sizes, oriented box and path tests, top-down order), actors from the world and lightweight instances from `AFGLightweightBuildableSubsystem`, measured by their meshes. Each batch goes to the state's own server call. `Config/AccessTransformers.ini` makes `FMKIAreaDismantle` a friend of the dismantle state.

## Dismantle unused supports

Dismantling a belt, conveyor lift, pipe or hypertube also dismantles the supports it leaves holding nothing (the maintainer, 2026-10-29): conveyor poles, stackable poles, conveyor wall and ceiling mounts, pipe supports (standing, stackable and wall) and hypertube supports. A support goes only when one of the dismantled belt's or pipe's ends rests on it and no other belt, lift, pipe or hypertube rests on it. Wall and floor holes always stay, and so does a stackable support with another one standing on it that stays.

- **Preview:** in the Default mode, the supports that would go are outlined red with the belt or pipe you aim at, and with the ones you have selected (multi-select), and the refund under the crosshair includes them.
- **Refund:** they are dismantled by the game's own dismantle with the rest, so the game checks each and refunds it to your inventory (then a dismantle crate), as if you had picked it.
- **Area mode** batches go through the same dismantle, so a support outside the cube that a taken belt left unused goes too.
- **Option:** **Dismantle Unused Supports** (default on) under Options > MKI Tools.

How it works: `FMKIOrphanSupports` finds the supports. A support is a building whose connections are all snap-only belt, pipe or hypertube points (wall holes, `AFGBuildablePassthroughBase` and anything named "Hole" excluded); a belt or pipe end rests on it when the end lies within 20 cm of one of its snap points (the same join Area mode uses), for the same kind of line. Hooks on the dismantle state's server calls (`Server_DismantleActors`, `Server_PeekAtDismantleRefund`) add the supports to what they are sent; an after-hook on its `TickState` outlines them when the aim or selection changes (the game's own outlines are drawn first). The first dismantles log `Unused supports:` lines in the MKITools log. `Config/AccessTransformers.ini` makes the module and `FMKIOrphanSupports` friends of the dismantle state.

## Auto pickup

Hold **E** for a second while looking at nothing E can use (the sky, the ground, a wall): a progress bar fills under the crosshair, and the game's own auto pickup turns on, with its "Auto-Pickup Active" indicator above the hotbar. While it is on, whatever pickable you look at (leaves, wood, mycelia, berries, nuts, mushrooms, flower petals, item pickups) is picked up every half second, through the game's own pickup (its rules, inventory checks and animation), and nothing happens while you look at nothing pickable. Hold E again to turn it off (while it is on, only a machine, container, vehicle or door under the crosshair keeps E the game's own). Looking at anything E can use while it is off (a machine, a bush, a pickup), E is the game's own, tapped or held.

The game has this auto pickup in its foliage pickup proxy (`AFGFoliagePickup`), but on keyboard and mouse nothing turns it on: its toggle action is bound only on a gamepad, and mapping E to it did nothing. `UMKIAutoPickupSubsystem` times the hold, draws its bar as a small Slate overlay, and calls the proxy's own `ToggleAutoPickUp` (the game's flag is the one state, and its indicator follows it). With that flag on, the game itself picks only while E is held, so while it is on MKI Tools runs the proxy's own pickup (`DoPickup`, as a press of E) on the pickable you look at: an item pickup, or the proxy's foliage instance when it lies on your view ray within reach (the proxy keeps its last target after you look away).

## Elevation and length readouts

The HUD shows how high things are, and how long (the maintainer, 2026-10-27):

- **Where you stand:** "Elevation 123.4 m" under the compass, top centre: the height of the player's feet (the bottom of the capsule).
- **While building:** under the crosshair, the elevation of the hologram, and where it has one its length: for belts, pipes, rails, hypertubes and MKI roads the length of the spline, with the elevation of the end being placed (the one that follows the aim); for conveyor lifts their height, with the elevation of their bottom; for power lines the game's own wire length (the one its maximum length is checked against) once the first end is set. Any other hologram shows its elevation (its location, where the game places it).
- **Heights** are the world height in metres (world Z / 100), the same frame MKI Bridge reports positions in; lengths and heights are in metres, one decimal.
- They hide while a menu, the map or a machine's window shows the cursor, and are switched off together by the option **Elevation and length readouts** (default on). Nothing in the world is changed; each client draws its own.

How it works: `UMKIHudReadoutsSubsystem` (a local tickable world subsystem) reads the player's capsule and, while the build gun is in its build state, the state's hologram each frame, and draws two lines as a small Slate overlay. `Config/AccessTransformers.ini` makes it a friend of `AFGWireHologram` for the wire's own length.

## Foundation area zoop

Always on, no option (the maintainer, 2026-10-29: a permanent tool). Foundations zoop as a rectangle as well as a line. After placing the first foundation, aim along and across to set the length and the width, each the nearest whole foundation to where the view ray crosses the hologram's level, held to the game's own zoop limit (`mMaxZoopAmount`, 9 more each way: 10 by 10) and to the directions the game blocks (a side snapped to another foundation). Aiming within half a foundation of the line gives a line. The game's foundation holograms are set to zoop only in a line (`mOnlyAllowLineZoop`), and clearing that flag alone still zooped a line (live), so after the game's own zoop update (`AFGFoundationHologram::SetZoopFromHitresult`) MKI Tools sets the zoop itself (`SetZoopAmount`; if the game's setter keeps it to a line, `mDesiredZoop` directly and `RegenerateZoopInstances`). Holograms, cost and building stay the game's own. The vertical zoop mode and ramps (their own zoop) keep the game's zoop. The first rectangles log `Area zoop:` lines in the MKITools log. While the zoop is a rectangle, the game's "Currently Building" count reads it along by across, "(10x10)", instead of its line count "(x10)": `UMKIAreaZoopLabelSubsystem` finds the game's text widget showing the line count on screen and rewrites it (the game writes its own count back when the zoop changes; the widget found is logged once, `Area zoop label:`).

## Options

MKI Tools' settings are under **Options > MKI Tools** in the game's menus, in their own tab next to the game's own (the maintainer, 2026-10-29). They look and work like the game's options: section headers (**Player**, **Items & Inventory**, **Building**, **Equipment**, **World**), the game's checkboxes and sliders, the description at the bottom when a row is hovered, search, **Apply** (a change waits for it, as the game's own do) and **Reset to Default**. The game builds its Options menu from cooked setting assets, so `UMKIToolsOptionsMenu` builds the settings in code (`UFGUserSetting`, the game's own apply cycle) and adds them to the game's user settings (`UFGGameUserSettings::mUserSettings`) once the game has set those up, checking once a second that they are still there. The values stay in SML's mod configuration (`UMKIToolsConfiguration`, saved in the game's config folder, so values saved before carry over): each setting starts from its SML value, an applied change is written back to it, and a change made elsewhere (the depot panel's checkbox) shows in the menu within a second. Mods > MKI Tools now shows the mod's details only. At startup, once, the Options menu's rows for the tab are built and counted (`Options self-test:` in the MKITools log).

The settings, in the menu's order:

- **Hand crafting multiplier** (whole numbers 1 to 20, default 1, key `HandCraftingSpeed`): how much crafting work each Craft Bench tick does, not how fast the ticks come. Each production step of the bench (`UFGWorkBench::Produce`) runs N times, and each can finish an item, so a tick finishes up to N items (the bench finishes at most one item per step; a single longer step did not help quick recipes). 1 leaves the game's own; a value typed outside 1-10 is held to the range.
- **Hand mining multiplier** (whole numbers 1 to 20, default 1, key `HandMiningSpeed`): how much ore each swing gives when mining a resource node by hand, not how fast the swings come. The swing animation paces the mining cycles (a shorter `mMiningCycleDuration` changed nothing live), so each completed cycle gives its reward (`AFGResourceMiner::Server_GiveMiningRewards`) N times.
- **Health regeneration speed** (whole numbers 1 to 20, default 1): how fast health regenerates on its own, as a multiple of the game's rate. The game's regeneration step (`AFGCharacterPlayer::TickHealthGeneration`) runs as if N times the time had passed; the wait after damage and the health curve (where regeneration stops) stay the game's own.
- **Movement speed bonus** (whole numbers 0 to 20, default 0; each step is 10%, so 10 is twice the game's speed and 20 three times; the maintainer found 2x still very fast and 10x ludicrous, and asked for more on 2026-10-25): how much faster the player moves sideways than the game's own, never up or down (the maintainer, 2026-10-23). It multiplies the player movement component's top speed (`GetMaxSpeed`: walking, sprinting, crouching, swimming, jumping and falling, flying) and its acceleration (`GetMaxAcceleration`, so the speed is reached as quickly and air control scales with it). Falling only caps the sideways speed; flying and swimming hold their vertical speed to the game's own (its top speed, or for flying its vertical flying speeds, whichever is higher) after each velocity step (`CalcVelocity`, `PhysFlying`). The hover pack and the parachute are the game's own movement modes with their own speeds: for each of their physics steps (`PhysCustom`) the hover pack's speed and acceleration, and the parachute's strafe top speeds, are multiplied and put back. The hover pack's vertical speed is then held to its own top speed (sprinting included), and the parachute's lateral-to-fall-speed conversion is divided by the same factor so the faster glide doesn't fall faster. Flying by a double jump (the game's Flight Mode or MKI Tools' option, not the cheat menu's ghost fly) moves at the ground speeds (the maintainer, 2026-10-29: "ground and flight speeds match each other"; live, ground sprint with the bonus was far faster than flying with Shift): for each flying physics step (`PhysFlying`) the game's flying top speeds are set to its ground ones times the bonus (`MaxFlySpeed` to `MaxWalkSpeed`, `mMaxFlySprintSpeed` to `mMaxSprintSpeed`, through the equipped Blade Runners' `GetAdjustedMaxSpeed` as the game's ground sprint is: live, their sprint boost left flying with Shift slower than sprinting with them on) and put back, with the `GetMaxSpeed` multiplier off meanwhile so the bonus counts once; this holds at bonus 0 too. The vertical speed stays the game's own flying one, and `Flight:` lines in the MKITools log give the speeds every few seconds while flying. Players only; creatures keep the game's own. Sliding (crouching at speed), ladders, hypertubes and ziplines keep the game's speed, and a slide entered at a multiplied speed is held to the game's top speed for it (live: it threw the player half across the map). In multiplayer every player needs the same setting, or the host corrects their movement.
- **Flight mode** (on/off, default off, key `FlightMode`; the maintainer, 2026-10-23): when on, a double jump starts flying and another ends it, as with the game's own Flight Mode advanced game setting; when off, a double jump doesn't start flying, whatever that setting says. It decides the player's `mFlyingIsToggleable` before each jump input (`Input_Jump`, `Jump`) and after the game's own update (`UpdateFlyingIsToggleable`). A player already flying can always double jump out, and turning the option off ends a flight (the player falls) within a second; the cheat menu's ghost fly is left alone.
- **Collect nearby items** (on/off, default off) and **Collect radius** (whole numbers 1 to 20, default 1 = 5 m; 20 = 100 m): automatically picks up, around the player, berries, nuts and bacon agaric (foliage whose pickup gives `Desc_Berry`, `Desc_Nut` or `Desc_Shroom`; not leaves, wood, flowers or mycelia), items lying on the ground (`AFGItemPickup`: materials around crash sites, power slugs, somersloops, mercer spheres, creature remains), and the contents of dismantle and death crates (`AFGCrate`, `CT_DismantleCrate` and `CT_DeathCrate`, as far as the inventory has room). Foliage and ground items go through the game's own foliage pickup proxy (`SetPickupData` / `SetItemToPickup`, then `DoPickup`), one every 0.1 s, as a press of E does; crates are emptied on the host. `UMKICollectNearbySubsystem`.
- **Elevation and length readouts** (on/off, default on, key `HudReadouts`): the player's elevation under the compass, and while building the hologram's elevation and length under the crosshair (see [Elevation and length readouts](#elevation-and-length-readouts)).
- **Short conveyor lifts** (on/off, default on): the game builds a conveyor lift down to a lower height only when it snaps between two connections stacked above each other (`mMinimumHeightWithVerticalConnection`); otherwise its shortest is `mMinimumHeight`. With this on, the shortest everywhere is the first whole step (`mStepHeight`, 1 m) at or above that lower height (1.5 m): 2 m, the vanilla short lift (live: 1.5 m put every height off the 1 m grid). The lift still extends in the game's own steps up to its own maximum. Set on the lift hologram before each update of its top (`AFGConveyorLiftHologram::UpdateTopTransform`).
- **Inventory slots** (a button, not a value): shows the current inventory size and how many slots MKI Tools has added, and a **+10 slots** button adds 10 per press, up to 500 added per save (on the host; the button needs a loaded save). Can only grow: `AMKIPlayerSubsystem` keeps the slots added in each save (saved), adds them to `GetTotalPlayerInventorySlots`, and grows every player inventory below that count (at once and every second). Nothing lowers the count, and a player's own inventory is never resized smaller (a hook on `UFGInventoryComponent::Resize`), so items in the extra slots are never lost, even before the save's slots are known. The game's settings have no button, so the row's control (`UMKIInventorySlotsController`) is built in code.
- **Fill Depot from inventory** (on/off, default off, key `FillDepotFromInventory`; the maintainer, 2026-10-28): also a checkbox in the inventory's Dimensional Depot panel, under the game's own "Take from inventory before Depot". When on, once a second, items in your inventory that the Dimensional Depot lists (has a row for, even at 0; an item with no row is never uploaded) go into your depot upload queue (the game's own upload inventory, which uploads them at its own pace), never more than the depot still has room for, counting what is queued already. Queued items of a kind the depot is full of (whoever queued them) come back to your inventory where there is room. An item whose depot count drops (taken out, or built or crafted from the depot) is left alone for a minute (`AMKIPlayerSubsystem::DepotCooldownSeconds`), so it can be used or equipped. Done on the host (`AMKIPlayerSubsystem`), for each player who has it on: each client sends its own option through the remote call object. The checkbox (`UMKIDepotFillSubsystem`) is added to each `BPW_CentralStorage_Inventory` panel the game creates, styled from the game's checkbox and label; the first one added logs the panel's widget tree.
- **Dimensional Depot list A-Z** (always on): the inventory's Dimensional Depot panel lists its items alphabetically by name, in the game's language (case ignored). When the game puts pinned items first, they stay first, A-Z among themselves. The list is sorted on the player's own screen whenever the game fills it (a new item, a pin, a search); nothing in the depot changes (`UMKIDepotFillSubsystem::SortLists`).
- **Chainsaw clearance radius** (whole numbers 1 to 20, default 1): how far around a sawn tree or bush the chainsaw also clears, as a multiple of the game's own radius (5 m, `mCollateralPickupRadius`): 1 is the game's own, 20 is 100 m. Applied each time the chainsaw is equipped. After the game's own clearing (`AFGChainsaw::RemoveCollateralFoliage`, which picks up only pickable plants and skips anything the chainsaw fells unless the chainsaw's area mode is on), MKI Tools clears the rest of the pickable and building-clearable foliage in the radius (such as short grass, without items) and, with "Chainsaw cuts boulders" on, the boulders, for their limestone together.
- **Object scanner range bonus** (whole numbers 0 to 20, default 0, key `ObjectScannerRangeBonus`; the maintainer, 2026-10-28): how much farther the object scanner reaches than the game's own range, in steps of 10%, so 10 is twice the range and 20 three times (+200%). Before each tick of the scanner (`AFGObjectScanner::Tick`) its detection range (`mDetectionRange`) is set to its class default times the multiplier, so a changed option applies at once. The scanner works out its signal from the distance over that range (whether the object is in range, the delay between beeps from `mBeepDelayMax` far away to `mBeepDelayMin` close by, and the screen's reading), so the reading scales with the range: it starts beeping slowly at the new range and beeps fastest only close to the object. The game's range and beep delays are logged once (`Object scanner:` lines in the MKITools log).
- **Chainsaw cuts boulders** (on/off, default on): the chainsaw cuts the boulders and rock clusters the game places as foliage (meshes under `/Game/FactoryGame/World/Environment/Rock/`), which the game otherwise leaves alone. MKI Tools answers yes to the game's own "is this foliage removable" check (`AFGFoliageRemovalSubsystem::IsRemovable`) for rock foliage only; bushes and trees keep the game's answer. The removal is the game's own: saved, synced in multiplayer, and permanent. With the option on, other things that clear removable foliage (such as building over it) clear boulders too.
- **Vegetation regrowth** (on/off, default off) and **Regrowth time (hours of play)** (whole numbers 1 to 20, default 3): cut trees, bushes and plants grow back at random, more likely the longer they have been gone. The game keeps every cut plant as a removed location in its foliage removal record (saved with the game). Once a minute the host gives each removed location a chance to grow back: a Weibull curve (shape 2) whose mean is the regrowth time, so few come back early and most around the average. The game keeps every foliage cell with cut plants loaded (live: 48 of 48), so a plant grows back into its cell straight away, but only more than 200 m from every player and, within 1 km, not in front of one. The record keeps only where the plant stood, so its yaw and size are drawn again from its foliage type; when the save next loads, the original plant is there exactly. In a cell that is not loaded, the plant just leaves the record. Boulders never regrow, nor does a spot within 3 m of a building. `AMKIRegrowthSubsystem` keeps its play-time clock and when it first saw each removed location in the save, and logs a summary every 10 minutes. In multiplayer, a client sees a regrown plant after reconnecting.

## Limestone

Removing rock gives limestone (`Desc_Stone`), on the server, once per removal, handed over through the game's own item pickup (dropped at the player and picked up, with the game's "+N" popup); what does not fit stays on the ground as an item drop:

- a boulder (Terrain mode or the chainsaw): 5 to 50, by its size;
- a level rock, arch or nobelisk rock (Terrain mode): 50 to 500, by its size. Restoring a rock through the bridge does not take the limestone back, and a removal made through the bridge gives none.

Boulders are the chainsaw's while "Chainsaw cuts boulders" is on (the maintainer's choice: the chainsaw shows the game's own pickup popup). With that option off, Terrain mode takes them instead: aimed at one, it is outlined, "Boulder: removal is permanent" shows under the crosshair, and holding fire removes it through the game's own foliage removal (`RemoveFoliageInstance`), so it cannot be restored.
